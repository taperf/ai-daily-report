## Latent Space — Building Visual Intelligence — Andreas Blattmann & Dustin Podell, Black Forest Labs

**The Takeaway:** 图像生成和机器人动作预测在数学上是同一件事，而 Black Forest Labs 现在的赌注是：让机器人看一次示范就能学会动作，他们判断这件事会在 2027 年被解决。

**一句话核心：** 图像生成和机器人动作预测在数学上是同一件事，而 BFL 现在的赌注是让机器人看一次示范就能学会动作，他们判断这件事 2027 年被解决。

Andreas Blattmann 是 latent diffusion 的共同发明人，那套技术后来被 Stable Diffusion 放大成了整个行业的地基；Dustin Podell 从洛杉矶的软件工作室辞职加入这场运动，一步步做到 SDXL 的创建者、Control LoRA、SD3 的后训练负责人，以及 Flux Ultra、Flux.1 和 Flux Context。这两个人凑在一起，等于把过去七年生成式图像的技术路线图当面讲了一遍。

最有价值的不是历史，是他们怎么解释技术选择的理由。SD3 那篇论文的核心问题听上去很朴素：从数据到噪声这条轨迹上，模型到底在哪一段学到了东西，算力应该花在哪一段？他们的答案是，均匀采样会在不重要的一段上浪费大量训练算力，需要过采样信息量大的区间。从经典 diffusion 迁移到 rectified flow，Andi 给的理由同样是把问题简化：diffusion 要求两个分布中必须有一个是标准正态，flow matching 不需要这个限制，数学上更一般，采样也更快。同样的逻辑用在 Flux Context 上，Dustin 的论证是模型已经学到了足够多的空间关系知识，只要有正确的数据和对预训练模型的正确训练方式，就能用很少的训练量唤出通用的图像编辑能力。

关于评测，Dustin 说得很直白：「play with your models」。他们的公开 Context Bench 有一半来自深夜在办公室里互相丢图片、输入最蠢的 prompt，「你得让人玩得开心」。Andi 补了一句更实用的：任何评测都有盲区，要把尽可能多独立评测拼起来看，但这不能免除你自己去采样模型、判断它的强项和弱项的责任，「这是开发模型的核心部分」。

真正的前瞻部分是机器人。他们的 Flux.3 是单一的模型，同时预测世界的状态和下一个动作，现在跑在大概 10 FPS，每次预测 9 到 21 帧的动作块，在真实机器人和仿真里都能用，computer use 被他们看作同一映射在数字世界的版本。Andi 说他们不打算走 fine-tuning 的路线，而是要在上下文学习上做到极致：现在的标准是四小时示范数据，而目标是让一个机器人「看一次任何动作，然后就能做」。「这才会是真正超人类的视觉智能。」他的时间点是 2027 年。

> **The Takeaway:** Image generation and robot action prediction are mathematically the same problem, and Black Forest Labs is now betting that a robot should learn an action after seeing it once, something they judge will be cracked in 2027.

> The most useful part is not the history but how they justify technical choices. The core question behind the SD3 paper sounds almost naive: along the trajectory from data to noise, which segment is the model actually learning from, and where should compute go? Their answer is that uniform sampling wastes training compute on segments that do not matter, so those should be undersampled and the informative regions oversampled. Moving from classic diffusion to rectified flow gets a similar simplification argument from Andreas Blattmann: diffusion requires one of the two distributions to be a standard normal, flow matching does not, so it is more general mathematically and samples faster. The same logic underwrites Flux Context, where Dustin Podell argues the model already carries enough spatial knowledge that the right data and the right training of a pretrained model can evoke general image editing with very little training.

On evaluation, Dustin Podell is blunt: play with your models. Half of their public Context Bench came from late nights in the SF office throwing images at each other and typing the stupidest prompts, because the models need to be fun to use. Andreas Blattmann adds the more practical point: every eval has blind spots, so combine as many independent ones as possible, but that does not remove your own responsibility to sample the model and find its strengths and weaknesses. That is a core part of developing a model.

The forward-looking part is robotics. Their Flux.3 is a single model predicting both world state and next action, running at roughly 10 FPS with chunked predictions of 9 to 21 frames, working on real robots and in simulation, with computer use treated as the same mapping in the digital world. Andreas Blattmann says they explicitly do not want to pursue fine-tuning but instead push context learning to the limit: four hours of demonstration data is the current standard, and the goal is a robot that sees any action once and can then do it. That, he says, would be genuinely superhuman visual intelligence. His date is 2027.
