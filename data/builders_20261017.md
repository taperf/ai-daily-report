### Jev: The AI That Makes Decisions, Not Text — and Why It Changes Everything — The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck（嘉宾 Diogo Almeida，TypeSafe CEO、RLHF 联合发明人）

**The Takeaway:** The next leap in AI may not be a smarter chatbot, but a radically cheaper and more reliable decision-maker embedded inside software: probabilities instead of prose.

Diogo Almeida is the CEO of TypeSafe, the company behind Jev, and a co-inventor of RLHF from his OpenAI days. One month after launch, Jev is the most copied model in AI: a launch video with 40M views, an $870M Series A at a $7.5B valuation, a trillion tokens a day, and a third of the Fortune 500. His framing of why chat models can't be automated: they are "95% Einstein, 5% Mr. Bean. And if it doesn't tell you when it's the Mr. Bean, then how are you gonna automate anything?"

Almeida argues that RLHF, his own creation, made models pleasing for chat but unreliable for decisions, producing sycophancy, mode dropping, over-refusal, and a warped probability space. Jev is what he calls "machine-native AI": System One decisions returned as calibrated probabilities with developer-set thresholds, composed in code like smart if-statements. Instead of programming a giant system prompt in natural language and praying, developers get a legible system where every decision can be measured, tuned, and trusted to run in the background. His slogan, worn on the launch video's t-shirt: "We're building prod, not God."

The contrarian takes come fast. He once wrote a document at OpenAI claiming "we could have had AGI last year," and still believes it. He calls pre-training "a giant dumpster fire of wasting resources" and says benchmarks are useless, hurting most the users they're meant to help. On the copycats (Cloudflare, Databricks, Microsoft, and OpenAI's own Decisions API): he built something very similar in his first week and found it embarrassingly unreliable; the hard, non-replicable part is reliability, earned through a new training method (RLCD) and new shapes of data that had to be invented in-house. He doesn't believe in recursive self-improvement either: "People see small percentage gains, and then they extrapolate it to the absolute moon," ignoring Amdahl's law. But the AI winter he feared is now averted in his view: markets may correct, but intelligence-per-dollar has dropped by orders of magnitude, and true to the Jevons Paradox that gave the model its name, cheap intelligence will end up consumed everywhere.

**核心观点：** AI 的下一次跃迁也许不是更聪明的聊天机器人，而是一种被嵌进软件里的、便宜得多也可靠得多的决策者：输出概率，而不是生成文本。

Diogo Almeida 是 TypeSafe 的 CEO、Jev 背后的推手，也是 OpenAI 时期 RLHF 的联合发明人。发布一个月来，Jev 已是 AI 界被模仿最多的模型：发布视频 4000 万播放、8.7 亿美元 A 轮（估值 75 亿美元）、每天一万亿 token、三分之一的财富 500 强在用。他解释为什么聊天模型没法自动化："95% 的爱因斯坦，5% 的憨豆先生。如果它不告诉你现在哪部分是憨豆，你怎么敢把任何事交给它自动化？"

他的核心论点：RLHF 这个他亲手参与发明的东西，让模型变成了擅长取悦人类但不可靠的决策者，带来谄媚、模式坍缩、过度拒答和被扭曲的概率空间。Jev 是他所说的"机器原生 AI"（machine-native AI）：System One 式的决策以校准概率的形式输出，阈值由开发者自己设定，再像聪明的 if 语句一样在代码里组合。开发者不再需要用自然语言写一个巨大的 system prompt 然后祈祷，而是得到一个完全可读的系统：每个决策都可以被测量、调优，并被信任在后台长期运行。他的口号就印在发布视频的 T 恤上："我们在造生产工具（prod），不是在造神（God）。"

逆主流观点接踵而至。他在 OpenAI 写过一份文档，声称"去年我们就可以实现 AGI"，至今仍然这么认为。他称预训练是"资源浪费的巨型垃圾箱"，说 benchmark 毫无用处，受害最深的恰恰是它们号称要帮助的那群用户。至于模仿者（Cloudflare、Databricks、微软，以及 OpenAI 自家的 Decisions API）：他自己在项目第一周就做出了类似的界面，结果可靠性差到尴尬；真正难以复制的部分是可靠性，来自全新的训练方法（RLCD）和必须在公司内部从零发明的全新形态的数据。他也不相信递归自我改进（RSI）："人们看到小幅的百分比提升，就把它外推到月球上去"，无视 Amdahl 定律的存在。但他原本担心的 AI 寒冬已经解除：市场也许会有回调，但单位美元的智能成本已经下降了几个数量级，而正如给模型命名的 Jevons 悖论所预言的那样，便宜的智能最终会被用在所有地方。
