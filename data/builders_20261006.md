**No Priors: Re-Founding Incumbents for the AI Era with Sequence Holdings Co-Founder and CEO Michael Lee**

The Takeaway: In the AI era, the highest-alpha move isn't founding a startup — it's acquiring the right incumbent, inheriting its brand, scale, network effects and regulatory moats, and refounding it around engineering culture and frontier AI.

Michael Lee co-founded Sequence Holdings twenty months ago on a contrarian bet: instead of deploying capital like a fund, the permanent holding company buys world-class incumbents, pairs them with a frontier engineering team (heavy Scale AI and Palantir DNA), and rebuilds the organization around what machines do best rather than speeding up the existing human assembly line. This month they announced the largest AI take-private to date: insurance brokerage Baldwin, for $7.7 billion with the Dell family office. Lee's filter for targets is unforgiving — "What we do is scarce. We're trying to do one deal per year" — and only in industries where the incumbent holds all the advantages (his example of what NOT to buy: outsourced coding services, where startups win). The sharpest part of his thesis is a cultural one: "Every company on the planet has a celebrated persona. In a world where you believe that alpha comes from engineering and AI, you need to create a culture whereby the celebrated persona is the engineer." That's why he believes Palantir exists — an organization that aggregates world-class engineers and wraps them for companies that can't recruit them. He rejects the services model (Accenture, McKinsey optimize for "getting in your wallet, staying in your wallet, growing the share of your wallet" — a path to incrementalism) and the software model (software must sell to workflows as they exist today). The bank pilot taught him that regulated, centralized businesses are ideal for agents: operations are well defined, data hygiene is excellent, and anything built at HQ amortizes across every branch. The hardest problem isn't the tech — "We have an engineering problem and a human engineering problem, and the human engineering problem is much more difficult than the engineering problem."

核心结论：在 AI 时代，超额回报最高的动作不是创业，而是收购对的在位者，继承它的品牌、规模、网络效应和监管护城河，然后用工程文化和前沿 AI 把它重造一次。

Michael Lee 在 20 个月前创立 Sequence Holdings，押注一个反共识命题：不做基金式撒钱，而是以永久持股公司的形式收购一流在位企业，配上前沿工程团队（团队带大量 Scale AI 和 Palantir 基因），围绕"机器最擅长什么"重新组织公司，而不是给现有的人类流水线提速。本月他们宣布了迄今最大的一笔 AI 私有化收购：联合 Dell 家族办公室以 77 亿美元买下保险经纪公司 Baldwin。Lee 的筛选标准苛刻到极致："我们做的事很稀缺，我们目标是每年只做一笔交易"，并且只投在位者占尽优势的行业（他的反例：外包编码服务，那是创业公司赢的领域）。他整套逻辑里最锋利的是文化命题："地球上每家公司都有一个被众星捧月的角色。当你相信 alpha 来自工程和 AI 时，你必须打造一种让工程师成为明星的文化。"这也是他对 Palantir 存在的解释：把顶尖工程师聚在一起，包装后卖给招不到这类人才的公司。他同时否定了服务模式（埃森哲、麦肯锡优化的是"进入你的钱包、留在你的钱包、扩大你钱包的份额"，通向渐进主义）和软件模式（软件只能卖给今天已存在的工作流）。银行的试点让他确信：受监管、中心化的企业反而是 agent 的理想土壤——运营规则清晰、数据卫生极好、总部建的任何东西都能摊薄到所有分支。而最难的不是技术："我们有一个工程问题和一个'人的工程'问题，后者比前者难得多。"
