**Thariq Shihipar（Anthropic Claude Code 工程师）**

Thariq laid out a direction for agent architecture: Claude's "brains" will increasingly live in the cloud, with "local hands" to operate on your computer. He unpacked one of the tricky problems — if Claude can only access your files while your machine is online, it may be effectively blocked until your computer is back on, so you need some kind of sync, and sync brings edge cases. He discussed this further on the Latent Space podcast. Separately, on the debate over coding agents eroding fundamentals, his take: working at a higher level of abstraction has always required understanding the lower levels — coding agents don't change that.

Thariq 给出了 agent 架构的一个明确方向：Claude 的「大脑」将越来越多地放在云端，同时给 Claude 一双「本地之手」来操作你的电脑。他剖析了其中的难点——如果 Claude 只能在你的电脑开机时访问文件，那它可能在你关机期间被卡住，所以需要某种同步机制，而同步又会带来各种边界情况。他在 Latent Space 播客里聊了更多。另外，针对「coding agent 会不会让人失去基本功」的讨论，他的观点是：在更高的抽象层工作从来都要求你理解底下一层——coding agent 改变不了这一点。

**Unsupervised Learning — Ep 94: Applied Compute CEO on the Limits of RL, the New AI Hyperscaler & Why Post-Training Wins Inference**

The Takeaway: RL is a hill-climbing machine — the hard part isn't the climbing, it's defining which hill to climb, and that makes your evals (and the experts behind them) the real competitive moat.

Applied Compute's CEO (ex-OpenAI, where he worked on Codex) builds the case for "owning your intelligence." The narrative isn't really about fearing malicious labs — it's about flexibility and control: where models run, how they're deployed, what you optimize them for. The trigger was open models getting good enough to actually do things with. And the risk is real: models have been pulled from products "more than once, particularly in the coding space," and as labs move into verticals, it's unclear they'll keep serving companies they compete against.

On post-training vs. harness optimization: start with the harness, but once you've squeezed it, you can still get a lot out of optimizing the policy itself — and the biggest inference workloads are exactly where post-training pays off first. His provocative frame: "Post-training wins inference." The most differentiated data is what's produced inside the company — judgment traces, how one firm's risk thresholds and operating model differ from another's. RL surprisingly hill-climbs even in non-verifiable domains if you convert them into proxy-verifiable ones (rubric-based RL against expert answers). Hence his sharpest warning: keep your evals private — "your employees are not fungible... same thing with these models."

The company's grand vision is a new AI hyperscaler for the GPU era — an inverted pyramid where training (few can do) sits below inference, routing, and context layers. And his contrarian market observation: Jevons' paradox is very real — price cuts trigger usage spikes on OpenRouter, and he underestimated how much enterprises would prioritize cost optimization over raw capabilities: "If I can do something 10x cheaper than my competitor, that is differentiation."

核心结论：RL 是一台爬山机——难点不在爬山，而在定义爬哪座山，这让你的 evals（以及 evals 背后的专家）成为真正的护城河。

Applied Compute 的 CEO（前 OpenAI 员工，主导过 Codex）系统论证了「拥有自己的智能」这件事。这个叙事的核心其实不是担心实验室作恶，而是灵活性与控制权：模型在哪里跑、怎么部署、针对什么优化。引爆点 是开放模型终于好用到能真正干活了。风险也是真实的：模型从产品中被下架的事「已经发生过不止一次，尤其是在 coding 领域」，而且随着各大实验室下场做垂直应用，它们会不会继续服务自己的直接竞争对手，很难说。

关于后训练 vs. harness 优化：先榨干 harness，但之后对策略（policy）本身的优化仍有很大空间——而最大的推理负载恰恰是后训练最先回本的地方。他有个很挑衅的框架：「Post-training wins inference」。最有差异化的数据是公司内部产生的——判断轨迹、一家公司的风险阈值和运营方式与别家有何不同。令人意外的是，RL 在不可验证的领域也能爬山，只要你把它转成代理可验证问题（用专家答案做 rubric-based RL）。由此他给出最尖锐的警告：把你的 evals 藏好——「你的员工不是可替换的……这些模型也一样」。

公司的宏大愿景是做 GPU 时代的新型 AI 超大规模云厂商——一个倒金字塔结构：训练（极少人能做）在最底层，往上是推理、路由、context 层。而他最反共识的市场观察：杰文斯悖论是真的——OpenRouter 上每次降价，用量就暴涨；他低估了企业对成本优化的执念会超过对纯粹能力的追求：「如果我能比竞争对手便宜 10 倍地做成同一件事，这就是差异化。」
