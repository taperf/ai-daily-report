### The Changing Physics of Cybersecurity and Drug Development — No Priors（嘉宾 Greg Brockman，OpenAI 联合创始人兼总裁）

**The Takeaway:** Brockman's bet is that security spend should "vastly outstrip" software spend, because as software becomes nearly free, reliability becomes the entire product — and the same logic that cracked mathematics will crack biology.

Greg Brockman, OpenAI's cofounder and president, sits down with Elad Gil and Regina to unpack the week: GPT-6 rolled out to over a billion people, and the math results published alongside it resolved close to 400 important open problems. His claim is blunt: whether AI can generate genuinely new knowledge "at this point, it's just impossible to deny." What excites him most is not the machine handing over answers, but the leverage on humans — "it's really what can a human equipped with these tools achieve that was unthinkable before."

The most contrarian position in the conversation is on cybersecurity. Brockman argues we are in a "defender's window": peak frontier AI can help defenders fix vulnerabilities before those capabilities diffuse to everyone. He reveals OpenAI put about 25 production engineers on hold from feature work to run its top model against its own infrastructure, fix what it found, and saturate — and notes a billion-dollar commitment to give frontline defenders access through the Daybreak program. His headline claim: "I have a feeling that cyber spend, security spend should actually vastly outstrip software spend," because when software itself becomes the cheap commodity, "you want software that is reliable, that is secure, that you can believe in." Formal verification, long too painful for anyone to use at scale, becomes practical when models are this good at proving things.

On drug discovery, he reasons from first principles: humans are "incredibly bad at biology," and a neural net has no reason to be better at natural language than at the language of biology — so expect breakthroughs, with the bottleneck shifting to data, wet labs, and even regulatory filings that AI can already compress. He also describes Dots, OpenAI's always-on proactive agent that watches his calendar, inbox, and Slack and surfaces what he forgot he promised — "a change to the form factor, not the capability." The episode closes on the iMac handle: Steve Jobs wanted people to feel they could control the machine, and Brockman wants the same for AI — humans as CEOs and board members, with one question left: "Are you happy? Is this what you wanted?"

**核心观点：** Brockman 押注的是，安全支出应该"远超"软件支出——因为当软件本身近乎免费，可靠性就成了产品的全部；而同样的逻辑，攻克了数学之后，下一个是生物学。

OpenAI 联合创始人兼总裁 Greg Brockman 与 Elad Gil、Regina 对谈，拆解了这一周：GPT-6 已推送给超过十亿用户，同期发布的数学成果解决了近 400 个重要公开问题。他的表态很直接：AI 能否生成真正的新知识，"到这个节点，已经没法否认了"。他最兴奋的不是机器给出答案，而是对人的杠杆效应——"真正的问题是人类拿着这些工具能做到什么，那些过去不可想象的事。"

整场对话最逆主流的判断在网络安全。Brockman 认为我们正处于"防御者窗口期"：最强的前沿 AI 能帮防御者赶在这些能力扩散到所有人之前修掉漏洞。他透露 OpenAI 让约 25 名生产工程师暂停手头项目，用自家最强模型对全部基础设施做安全扫描，修完发现的问题直到"饱和"；还有一项十亿美元级承诺，通过 Daybreak 计划把模型开放给一线防御者使用。他的标题级断言："我有一种感觉，网络安全支出应该远超软件支出。"因为当软件本身变成廉价大宗商品，"你要的是可靠、安全、让你信得过的软件"。过去贵到没人用得起的形式化验证，在模型这么擅长证明的当下变得可行了。

关于药物研发，他从第一性原理推演：人类"在生物学上差得离谱"，而神经网络没有理由在自然语言上比在"生物学的语言"上更强——所以突破必然到来，瓶颈会转移到数据、湿实验室，甚至 AI 已经能大幅压缩的监管申报文件上。他还介绍了 Dots，OpenAI 那个永远在线的主动式 agent：盯着他的日历、收件箱和 Slack，提醒他忘了兑现的承诺——"这是形态的变化，不是能力的变化"。节目最后回到 iMac 的提手：乔布斯想让用户感到自己能掌控机器，Brockman 对 AI 抱有同样的期待——人来做 CEO 和董事会，最后只剩一个问题："你满意吗？这是你想要的吗？"
