### No Priors: Redefining Chip Architecture with Arm CEO Rene Haas

**The Takeaway:** Even in the AI accelerator boom, the humble CPU remains the irreplaceable orchestrator of computing, and AI is now eating the most time-consuming part of chip design itself: verification.

No Priors sat down with Rene Haas, CEO of Arm and SoftBank Group International, at a moment when chip architecture is back at the center of the AI conversation. Haas's core argument is that no matter how much AI inference moves to GPUs and custom accelerators, "there's no computing problem that's ever been invented that doesn't utilize the microprocessor... All roads lead through it." CPUs are what orchestrate and arbitrate where tokens go, which makes Arm's IP licensing business (in everything from smartphones to data centers) a tollbooth on AI itself.

The most counterintuitive insight concerns how chips actually get made. Chip design takes 24 to 36 months, but the design itself is not the bottleneck: "The largest amount of time is in the verification, the validation, the debug. AI is really good at that." Haas compares shutting off AI tooling in chip development to having the internet in the 1990s but only being allowed online between two and four in the morning. His conclusion: "The genie's out of the bottle and there's no stopping that."

The episode also explains Arm's strategic evolution. For decades Arm only licensed IP; a few years ago it moved to compute subsystems, which Haas describes with a Lego analogy, "providing the blueprint on how to stitch it all together." Demand was insane. The final leap was physical chips, triggered by Meta, which wanted a general-purpose agentic CPU nobody else could provide and asked Arm to build it together. Haas says the ecosystem, including Nvidia, Amazon, Microsoft, and Google, all of whom build Arm-based server chips, accepted the move with less pushback than expected. Arm remains deliberately fabless with no intention of building a fab, relying on TSMC and Samsung while borrowing leadership from Broadcom, Qualcomm, and Nvidia to build supply chain muscle it never needed when the business was pure licensing with its famous 98.5% gross margins.

**要点提炼：** 即便在 AI 加速器狂潮中，CPU 仍是计算中不可替代的调度中枢，而 AI 正在吃掉芯片设计中最耗时的环节：验证。

本期 No Priors 对话 Arm 兼软银集团国际 CEO Rene Haas，正值芯片架构重回 AI 议题中心的时刻。Haas 的核心论点是：无论 AI 推理多少迁移到 GPU 和定制加速器上，「从来没有一种计算问题不需要微处理器……条条大路都经过它」。CPU 负责调度和仲裁 token 的去向，这让 Arm 的 IP 授权业务（覆盖从手机到数据中心的一切）成了 AI 时代的收费站。

本期最反直觉的洞察关乎芯片制造的真相：芯片设计需要24到36个月，但设计本身不是瓶颈，「最耗时间的是验证、确认和调试，而这恰恰是 AI 最擅长的」。Haas 把在芯片开发中关掉 AI 工具比作「90年代就有互联网，却只允许凌晨两点到四点上网」。他的结论是：「精灵已经放出瓶子，谁也拦不住了。」

本期还解释了 Arm 的战略演变：几十年来 Arm 只做 IP 授权；几年前转向 compute subsystems，Haas 用乐高类比，「提供把所有部件拼起来的蓝图」，需求火爆；最后一步是做物理芯片，导火索是 Meta，他们想要一颗通用 agentic CPU 而市面上无人能提供，于是找 Arm 合作。Haas 表示，包括 Nvidia、Amazon、Microsoft、Google 这些都做 Arm 架构服务器芯片在内的生态伙伴，对此的反对比预想少得多。Arm 刻意保持 fabless 模式、不打算自建晶圆厂，依赖 TSMC 和 Samsung 代工，并从 Broadcom、Qualcomm、Nvidia 挖来高管补供应链能力。要知道，纯 IP 授权时代的 Arm 拥有著名的 98.5% 毛利率，什么都不用管。
