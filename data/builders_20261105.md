### 《The Inside Story of the 50 Who Built Modern AI With Kevin Roose》— AI & I by Every

**The Takeaway:** 我们不会完蛋，但前提是「别等到出事才监管」——AI 行业现在缺的是一道药品上市前的安全测试关卡。

**EN:** Kevin Roose spent a decade inside the labs as a technology reporter, then went back and interviewed roughly 50 of the people who actually built modern AI — 150 in total — for The AGI Chronicles, now a New York Times bestseller. His verdict, stated plainly: "I don't think we're doomed. But we have a lot to do." The useful part is not the reassurance but the mechanism he proposes. Asked what would make Washington act, he is cold-eyed: "I think it's clear that we're not going to get federal regulation of AI, at least right now from the Trump administration. I think what would need to happen for them to take a more active role is for something bad to happen." His preference is that industry preempts the crisis: "We require pharmaceutical companies to test their drugs before selling them to the public. We should require AI companies to test their models before offering them to the public." From there, "some kind of a coordination mechanism for slowing down the frontier" — a position he says he reversed on in recent months, having previously assumed acceleration was unstoppable.

The most exportable idea in the conversation has nothing to do with policy. Roose divides every task in a working day into two buckets: **weightlifting** and **forklifting**. Weightlifting tasks are ones where "the struggle is part of the benefit" — bringing a forklift into the gym is a "total mismatch of tool and method." Forklifting tasks are heavy loads moved from point A to point B, where a tool that shortens the path should absolutely be used. He applies the frame to his own life without hedging: he weightlifts parenting ("You cannot outsource that. It is not good for you. It is not good for the child"), and forklifts the legal and insurance paperwork of starting a company.

On method, he refuses to hand over the writing — "all the words are from this brain and these fingers" — but built a "council of Claudes" in Claude Code, assigning each agent a persona (an AI skeptic, an AI historian, a Walter Isaacson style craftsman), with a master editor agent synthesizing their notes. It caught factual errors his human editors missed. His line on that shift is the one worth stealing: "A couple years ago, we had to fact check the AIs, and now the AIs fact check me."

**中文：** Kevin Roose 做了十年科技记者，一直在实验室的房间里；后来他回过头，采访了约 50 位真正造出当代 AI 的人（连同周边共 150 人），写成《The AGI Chronicles》，如今已是《纽约时报》畅销书。他的结论说得很直白：「我不认为我们注定完蛋。但我们有很多事要做。」有价值的部分不是这份安慰，而是他给出的机制：被问到什么才会让华盛顿动手时，他毫不乐观——「很明显，我们不会拿到联邦层面的 AI 监管，至少现在这届政府不会。要让他们更积极，得先出事。」而他希望行业抢在危机前面：「我们要求制药公司在卖给公众之前先测试药品。我们也应当要求 AI 公司在向公众提供模型之前先测试模型。」再往前一步，是「某种让前沿减速的协调机制」——他坦言这是近几个月才转变的立场，此前他认为加速根本停不下来。

整场对话里最可迁移的东西与政策无关。Roose 把一天中的所有任务分成两类：**举重**和**开叉车**。举重型任务的特点是「挣扎本身就是收益」，把叉车开进健身房是「工具和方法的彻底错配」；叉车型任务是重物从 A 点搬到 B 点，这时任何能缩短路径的工具都该用。他丝毫不含糊地把它套在自己身上：育儿是他举的重量——「这件事没法外包。外包对你不好，对孩子也不好」；开公司那些法律和保险文书，他交给机器。

方法上，他拒绝让 AI 代笔——「每一个字都出自这个大脑和这双手」——但在 Claude Code 里搭了一个「Claude 议会」：给每个 agent 分配一个人格视角（AI 怀疑论者、AI 史学家、Walter Isaacson 式的文字匠人），再由一个总编 agent 汇总意见。它抓出了他和人类编辑都漏掉的事实错误。他对这个变化的概括值得直接引用：「几年前是我们核查 AI，现在是 AI 核查我。」
