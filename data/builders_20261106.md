### Unsupervised Learning — Ep 97: Jev Creator on Decision Models, Benchmark Fatigue & Why the Labs Missed It

**嘉宾：** Jevons AI 的 CEO（一个月前其发布引发行业热议，播客录制于发布数周后，因此有了回看的空间）。

**EN — The Takeaway:** If frontier models were already at PhD level on hard science questions back in 2023, the fact that most knowledge work is still done by humans is not a capability gap, it is a deployment gap, and benchmarks have made it harder to see.

His core diagnosis is that the industry keeps measuring intelligence on a single axis, and the labs are optimizing for demos rather than reliability. The provocation comes early and stays sharp: "if they are so unbelievably good at software, like, why do normal software engineers have anything to do? Not that I want them automated, but, like, they clearly aren't, and there's clearly something missing there." He is equally blunt about measurement: "I think intelligence as this single axis that is measured by whatever the benchmark of the day is is, like, totally wrong."

On where he thinks the real opportunity sits, the argument is architectural rather than aspirational. Most tokens consumed today are shaped for human consumption, where style and prose matter; he believes the future is machine native, where an LLM is a submodule called by software. "JEV is not yet there," he concedes, but working backwards from that cognitive core is what drives his team. He applies the same logic to multimodality with an unfashionable honesty: multimodality tends to degrade intelligence, measurably so, and "my philosophy has always been automate the easy work before the hard work."

On competition, he is almost dismissive: "It feels like other people are digging our moat for us, which is kind of not how I thought it would go, and I think that that's kind of bad for the field," warning that benchmark-optimized releases burn the goodwill that actually matters. On the business, he says the company "can run our service profitably" and hopes never to subsidize for lock-in, calling that "a very shadowy, sneaky way to get people." On AI safety he refuses the monolith: ASI discourse, bio risk, cyber risk and trust and safety are different problems, and he thinks most people worried about ASI are "a little bit divorced from the reality of the technology." On Anthropic's outlook he lands the most quotable line of the day: sitting expectations that AI will simply be allowed to cure cancer will feel like "Marie Antoinette's, like, let them eat cake."

**中文：** **核心结论：** 如果前沿模型早在 2023 年就在高难度科学问答上达到了博士水平，那么今天大部分知识工作仍由人来做，就不是能力问题，而是部署问题；而 benchmark 恰恰让这个差别更难被看见。

他的核心诊断是，行业一直把智能当成单一轴来测量，而实验室在优化演示，而不是优化可靠性。这个挑衅来得很早，也一直很锋利：「如果它们真的在软件上强到难以置信，那普通软件工程师为什么还有活干？我不是希望他们被自动化，而是很显然它们并没有做到，这里面显然缺了东西。」他对度量的批评同样直接：「把智能当成一条单一轴、用当天流行的某个 benchmark 来衡量，是完全错误的。」

关于机会在哪里，他的论证是架构层面的，而不是愿景层面的。今天被消费的 token 大多是为人消费而设计的，因此风格、文风才重要；他相信未来是机器原生（machine native）的，LLM 只是被软件调用的一个子模块。「JEV 还没有到那一步，」他承认，但从那个「认知内核」倒推，正是他团队的动力。他把同样的逻辑用在不那么讨喜的多模态判断上：多模态会降低智能，而且这一点是可测量的；「我的哲学一直是，先自动化容易的工作，再动难的工作。」

谈到竞争，他几乎是不屑的：「感觉是别人在替我们挖护城河，这和我原本想的走向不一样，而且我认为这对整个领域是坏事。」他警告说，比真实世界更偏 benchmark 优化的模型发布，会烧掉用户愿意尝试的那点善意，而那是唯一重要的东西。关于生意，他说公司「可以盈利地运营这项服务」，并且希望永远不必为了锁定用户去临时补贴，称那是一种「很阴暗、很偷偷摸摸的做法」。关于 AI 安全，他拒绝把它当成铁板一块：ASI 讨论、生物风险、网络安全风险和信任与安全是不同的东西，他认为多数担心 ASI 的人「和技术现实有点脱节」。谈到 Anthropic 的估值与前景，他给出了今天最值得引用的一句：如果期待里有「让它们去治愈癌症」这种假设，那这句话最终会像「玛丽·安托瓦内特的『让他们吃蛋糕』」一样。
