### Anthropic Engineering: An update on recent Claude Code quality reports

Anthropic published a detailed postmortem on reports that Claude's responses had worsened for some users. They traced the issue to three separate changes affecting Claude Code, the Claude Agent SDK, and Claude Cowork — the API itself was not impacted.

The three issues:
1. **March 4**: Changed default reasoning effort from high to medium to reduce latency. Reverted April 7 after users preferred higher intelligence.
2. **March 26**: A caching optimization meant to clear old thinking from idle sessions had a bug that caused it to clear thinking on every turn, making Claude seem forgetful and repetitive. Fixed April 10.
3. **April 16**: A system prompt instruction to reduce verbosity ("keep text between tool calls to ≤25 words") hurt coding quality and was reverted April 20.

Anthropic noted that the aggregate effect looked like broad degradation because each change affected different slices of traffic on different schedules. As remediation, they're resetting usage limits for all subscribers and tightening controls on system prompt changes, including broader eval suites and gradual rollouts.

> "We never intentionally degrade our models, and we were able to immediately confirm that our API and inference layer were unaffected."

### Anthropic Engineering: Scaling Managed Agents

Anthropic detailed the architecture behind Managed Agents, their hosted service for long-horizon agents. The core design principle: decouple the "brain" (Claude and its harness) from the "hands" (sandboxes and tools) and the "session" (the event log).

Key architectural decisions:
- **Cattle, not pets**: By moving the harness out of the container, failed containers become recoverable tool-call errors rather than lost sessions
- **Security boundary**: Tokens are never reachable from the sandbox where Claude's generated code runs — Git access tokens are wired into remotes during initialization, and MCP tools are called via a proxy that fetches credentials from a secure vault
- **Session as external context**: The session log lives outside Claude's context window, allowing the brain to interrogate history via `getEvents()` without irreversible compaction decisions
- **Performance gains**: Decoupling dropped p50 TTFT by roughly 60% and p95 by over 90%, since inference can start before containers are provisioned

The post draws an analogy to operating systems: just as OSes virtualized hardware into abstractions (process, file) that outlasted the hardware, Managed Agents virtualize agent components into interfaces meant to outlast any particular implementation.

### No Priors: Rethinking Legacy Data Infrastructure with Eon Co-Founders

**Ofir Ehrlich and Gonen Stein, Co-founders of EON** — EON is a cloud backup and disaster recovery service designed for the AI era.

**The Takeaway**: In the AI era, enterprise data has transformed from a liability (tapes on shelves) into the single most valuable and sought-after asset a company owns — but unlocking it requires entirely new infrastructure that can map, classify, secure, and make sense of data scattered across decades of legacy systems.

The conversation covers why Google paid $10 million for Spirit Airlines' data out of bankruptcy (not the planes — the data), with multiple AI companies including Mercor bidding on the dataset. As Stein put it: "Models, compute, everything is relatively ephemeral, almost zero switching costs... the most valuable thing that you have is actually your data."

A major thread is AI security. Ehrlich contrasted traditional human threats (ransomware) with the new threat from "non-human actors, agents that essentially have legitimate access to the environment." The velocity of AI-caused incidents is "extreme." Stein noted that six months ago no one discussed this, but now "pretty much every person I meet... tells me either they are afraid of that happening to them or it personally happened."

On the enterprise stack, Stein argued we'll actually see *more* dashboards, not fewer, because tracking agent chains becomes "almost an impossible task" as "agents activating other agents activating other agents" proliferate. He also highlighted how non-technical employees using tools like Lovable are creating "a complete set of actors inside the organization, not bound by the rules of the organization."

The discussion touches on how AI is changing software consumption — from forward-deployed engineers helping legacy enterprises adopt AI, to companies buying other companies specifically to acquire their data for training.

> "We live in very interesting times." — Gonen Stein
