### 2. Vercel CEO Guillermo Rauch — 给模型做一条 DNS

**EN:** Rauch framed Vercel's AI Gateway model aliasing as infrastructure, not convenience: "This is like DNS for models. An alias that *resolves* to a model, giving you traffic-steering abilities. Solves a huge number of problems. From not having teams stuck on old model versions, to saving on costs, to removing 'decision paralysis'. Try it!" His diagnosis of the actual failure mode is worth noting — the problem isn't picking a model, it's that organizations freeze once a model is written into a codebase. An indirection layer turns model choice into a runtime concern. In the same session he announced Vercel AI Gateway will serve Particle's models and inference, and laid out a three-part answer to the "AI coding bottleneck": fast, stable CI infrastructure (20 worldwide clusters, millions of microVMs per day), Turborepo's dependency-graph caching, and agentic CI where agents can inspect, abort, trigger and replay CI from the CLI.

**中文：** Rauch 把 Vercel AI Gateway 的模型别名机制定位成基础设施，而不是便利功能：“这就像给模型做 DNS。一个*解析*到某个模型的别名，给你流量调度的能力。解决了大量问题——从团队被卡在旧模型版本上，到省钱，到消除'决策瘫痪'。”他对真实故障模式的判断值得注意：问题不是选不出模型，而是一旦某个模型写进了代码库，组织就冻住了。加一层间接寻址，把模型选择变成运行时的事。同一次他还宣布 Vercel AI Gateway 将托管 Particle 的模型与推理，并给出“AI 编程瓶颈”的三段式解法：快速稳定的 CI 基础设施（全球 20 个集群，每天数百万个 microVM）、Turborepo 的依赖图缓存，以及 agentic CI——agent 可以从命令行检查、中止、触发、重放 CI。

### 7. Box CEO Aaron Levie — Agent 会消耗今天 1000 倍的 token

**EN:** Levie's forecast: "Agents are going to consume 1,000X more tokens than we consume today. Because they're going to be running in our workflows, processing all of our data, building out nearly all software, securing enterprises, and much more." The strategic conclusion is not "more closed-model scaling" — it's portfolio diversification: "continuing to push the boundaries of closed models, while also increasing the capability of open weights models, making them more secure, as well as making them more efficient for large workloads." Note this lands days after his own datapoint that enterprise agent deployment sits under 1% — the gap between 1% and 1000x is the entire thesis.

**中文：** Levie 的预测：“Agent 会消耗比今天多 1000 倍的 token。因为它们会跑在我们的工作流里、处理我们全部的数据、写掉几乎所有的软件、替企业做安全，还有更多。”他的战略结论不是“继续加大闭源模型”，而是组合分散：“继续推闭源模型的边界，同时提升开源权重模型的能力、让它们更安全，也让它们在大负载下更高效。”1000 倍的需求曲线意味着单位经济性成为硬约束，这也是他话锋一转去背书 Particle 的原因。注意这条距离他自己“企业 agent 部署率不到 1%”的判断只有几天——1% 和 1000 倍之间的差距，就是整个论证。

### 8. Cursor / Notion / Stripe 设计师 Ryo Lu — 计算机不是为了替你做工作

**EN:** Lu wrote the anti-factory manifesto of the day, tracing his position back to Engelbart's 1962 "augmenting human intellect" framing: "When I started making software, I wasn't dreaming of a factory... The computer wasn't interesting because it could do work without me. It was interesting because of what I could do with it." His worry is that agent-first design quietly replaces the tool-for-thinking with the tool-for-output, and the thinking is where the value was. Worth reading as a design brief rather than nostalgia — the question he raises is what an agentic interface looks like when its success metric is the quality of the user's reasoning, not the speed of the artifact.

**中文：** Lu 写了今天最像“反工厂宣言”的一段，把自己的立场一路追溯到 Engelbart 1962 年的“增强人类智能”：“我开始做软件的时候，梦想的不是一座工厂……计算机之所以有意思，不是因为它能替我把活干了，而是因为我能用它做什么。”他担心的是，agent 优先的设计会悄悄把“用来思考的工具”换成“用来出活的工具”，而价值恰恰长在思考那一段。这段该当作设计纲要来读，而不是怀旧——他提的问题是：如果一个 agent 界面的成功指标是用户推理的质量而不是交付物的速度，它该长什么样。

### AI & I by Every — 《Can You Use AI To Make Good Art? — With Fred Graver》

**The Takeaway：** AI 不能给你“要说什么”，只能帮你把已经有的东西说得更好——所以打开模型之前，先花一天把你自己倒空。

**EN:** Fred Graver has a career that reads like a controlled experiment in chasing the audience. He started as a copy editor at National Lampoon (hired because he could type 100 words a minute and wouldn't ruin the joke), wrote for Letterman, worked with Norman Lear, wrote on Cheers and In Living Color, then heard a modem and went to Disney's Imagineers, MTV Networks, and became head of TV at Twitter in its second year — the era that produced the Ellen selfie. From there he became a conversation designer at Microsoft, writing bots with the AI teams.

His thesis is that AI belongs in the creative process as a **virtual writers' room, not as a ghostwriter.** The method has an order that matters. First, empty out: “You have to start with uniquely you, what you know. Spend a day or two dumping everything you know about what you're going to do onto a page. Do it by hand... But empty out. You will never have that opportunity again once you open AI.” Second, build a “who I am as a writer” document plus a “what I'm working on” document, so the model has a persona to reason against. Third, ask the three questions: does this idea have anything to do with this person; is there an audience out there; what will I need to get to a finished script that I don't have now.

He maps producing onto three phases — ideation/architecture, production, and reaching the audience — and argues the whole thing should be a circle rather than Henry Ford's assembly line: marketing people sitting in during pre-production, testing a proxy trailer before the thing even exists. In Hollywood, marketing is already the most AI-native department, rewriting trailers hour by hour against intent-to-purchase data.

The counterintuitive part is his answer to “will AI produce slop.” His analogy is Annie Leibovitz: everyone now carries a camera better than the one she used for thirty years, yet very few people take pictures like she does. The tool is not the differentiator; the point of view is. Claude can generate a full pitch for a web series about 70-year-olds running a retirement home's Facebook page — his example, drawn from a session where he'd fed it his own taste — but it will not supply the small improvised detail that makes a character lovable, the way the voice of Homer Simpson saying “Don't” was improvised on the spot.

He is blunt about the part nobody has solved. The first two phases are coming into place. The third — how a creator reaches an audience and gets paid once the golden-age television ladder is gone — is broken: “As Bruce Springsteen would say, boys, those jobs ain't coming back.” His open question is whether Kevin Kelly's thousand true fans can be institutionalized for people who make things. And his practical kit is small and specific: a skill that builds a pitch deck using Pixar's “and therefore” structure and refuses to advance until each step is done; a deliberately unpleasant morning check-in agent that calls out the new idea he started at midnight when he owed yesterday's work; Claude as the long-term room, ChatGPT as the film-school student who has read every guide without knowing what it means, and NotebookLM as the podcast hosts who love everything he writes.

**中文：** Fred Graver 的职业生涯读起来像一场关于“追着观众跑”的对照实验。他在 National Lampoon 做校对起步（被录用是因为一分钟能打 100 个词、而且不会把笑话改坏），后来给 Letterman 写稿、跟 Norman Lear 共事、参与《Cheers》和《In Living Color》，然后听到了调制解调器的声音，去了迪士尼幻想工程、MTV 电视网，在推特的第二年做了电视业务负责人——那个产出“艾伦自拍”的年代。之后他到微软做对话设计，和 AI 团队一起写机器人。

他的主张是：AI 在创作过程中扮演的角色是**虚拟编剧室，而不是代笔**。方法有顺序，顺序本身是重点。第一步，倒空：他说“你必须从独特的你、你知道的东西开始。花一两天，把所有你关于这件事知道的东西倒到纸上。用手写……但一定要倒空。一旦你打开 AI，你就再也没有这个机会了。”第二步，建立一份“作为写作者我是谁”的文档，加一份“我正在做什么”的文档，让模型有一个可以对之推理的人格参照。第三步，问三个问题：这个想法和这个人有关系吗；外面有没有观众；要拿到一份完成的剧本，我还缺什么。

他把制片映射成三个阶段——构思/架构、制作、触达观众——并主张整件事应该是一个圆环，而不是亨利·福特的流水线：营销人员在前期筹备阶段就坐在里面，正片还不存在时就先做一版代理预告片去试票房。在好莱坞，营销已经是 AI 渗透最深的部门，会按“购票意向”数据逐小时改写预告片。

最反直觉的是他对“AI 会不会只能生产垃圾”的回答。他的类比是 Annie Leibovitz：如今每个人口袋里的相机都比她用了三十年的那台好，但很少有人能拍出她那样的照片。工具不是差异化的来源，观点才是。Claude 能生成一部“70 岁老人运营养老院 Facebook 主页”的网剧完整企划——他举的例子，来自一次他把自己品味喂给模型的会话——但它给不出那个让角色可爱的小即兴细节，就像荷马·辛普森的配音演员当场即兴的那句“Don't”。

对没人解决的那一块，他很直接。前两个阶段正在成型。第三个阶段——在电视黄金时代的梯子消失之后，创作者如何触达观众并挣到钱——是断的：“用 Bruce Springsteen 的话说，孩子们，那些工作不会回来了。”他悬而未决的问题是：Kevin Kelly 的“一千个真粉丝”，能不能为做东西的人制度化。他手上的工具很具体：一个用皮克斯“因为、所以”结构生成提案 deck 的 skill，每一步没做完就不许进下一步；一个被他要求“非常不客气”的早晨打卡 agent，专门抓他半夜冒出来的新点子；他把 Claude 当长期待的那个房间，ChatGPT 当读过所有指南却不懂其意涵的电影学院学生，NotebookLM 当那对什么都夸你的播客主持人。
