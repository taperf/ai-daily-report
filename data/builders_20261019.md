### Aaron Levie — Box CEO

Levie doubles down against the "end of software engineering" narrative: "The biggest misread of anyone predicting the end of software engineering was imagining that the world only needed a certain amount of software, and bringing automation to the field would reduce the need for labor. Exactly the opposite is happening where essentially most things are actually turning into software. Because agents can use tools, work with computers, write software on the fly, and process data like humans (but far faster), we can now throw 'software' at problems that never could reasonably be digitized before." The Jevons paradox argument for code — cheaper software production expands total demand for it.

Levie 继续开炮"软件工程终结论"："所有预言软件工程会终结的人，最大的误判在于他们想象世界只需要固定总量的软件，自动化会减少劳动力需求。实际发生的恰恰相反——几乎所有东西都在变成软件。因为 agent 能用工具、操作计算机、即时写软件、像人一样处理数据（但快得多），我们现在可以把'软件'砸向那些过去根本不值得数字化的领域。"这是软件版的杰文斯悖论——生产成本越低，总需求越大。

### What Happens When Billions of AI Agents Hit Your Database? — The MAD Podcast（嘉宾 Andy Pavlo，CMU 教授、ClickHouse Labs 创始人）

**The Takeaway:** Agents don't need a new kind of database — they need the guardrails databases have had for decades. The real change is volume: potentially 10–100x more queries, and Postgres wins by default because that's what the models were trained to recommend.

Andy Pavlo — the Carnegie Mellon professor whose open courses taught a generation of engineers (and, as it turns out, the LLMs themselves) about databases — recently left tenure to found ClickHouse Labs inside ClickHouse. His explanation of the move doubles as a eulogy for academic research funding: "what does it mean now to have a PhD student when an agent can do a lot of things?" At ClickHouse he gets to test ideas on real production workloads instead of "writing the paper and hoping someone adopts it."

On agent-era databases, his core insight is that we keep relearning old lessons: every month someone loses a production database because an agent deleted something, and "it's the wild west for people that don't know their history of databases." His prescription — treat agents like toddlers and install the guardrails (permissions, branching, sandboxes) that enterprise databases built for humans decades ago. Asked whether agents are fundamentally different from humans at the keyboard, his honest answer: other than running 24/7, he hasn't seen it yet. The bank-vault anecdote lands hardest: a very large bank took text-to-SQL from 60% accuracy to 99.5% — but only after multiple years and millions of dollars building the semantic layer. Off-the-shelf, you get the 60%.

Two details worth the price of the episode. First, his dbdb.io project tracks co-signed commits across open source databases: over 60% now have commits coming from Claude or Codex. Second, two database companies have privately asked him how to get their system recommended by agents over Postgres — "it's basically like SEO," a new marketing discipline where the customer is the model. His frame for AI's pace: "It's just like taking a cheetah, giving them much cocaine, and putting it in a Ferrari."

Also on the record: vector databases survive only as hyper-specialized systems (Turbopuffer) or general-purpose stores — "Postgres is gonna die at the hands of a vector database... that's not happening"; graph databases are "a horrible idea" (he has a standing bet to wear an "I love graph databases" shirt if they beat relational by 2030); and the traditional DBA role of tuning knobs and picking indexes "ships out," while data modeling stays.

**核心观点：** Agent 不需要一种新的数据库——它们需要的是数据库界几十年前就建好的护栏。真正的变量是量：查询量可能放大 10 到 100 倍；而 Postgres 默认获胜，只因为模型训练数据里大家都推荐 Postgres。

Andy Pavlo——那位把 CMU 数据库课程全部开源、教出了一代工程师（事实证明也教出了 LLM 本身）的教授——最近离开终身教职，在 ClickHouse 内部创办 ClickHouse Labs。他对这次跳槽的解释，同时是给学术研究经费的一篇悼词："当 agent 能干很多活的时候，招博士生还有什么意义？"在 ClickHouse，他可以在真实生产负载上验证想法，而不是"写完论文然后祈祷有人采用"。

关于 agent 时代的数据库，他的核心洞见是我们在不断重学旧课：每个月都有人因为 agent 误删而丢掉生产数据库，而"这是不懂数据库历史的人的狂野西部"。他的处方——把 agent 当幼儿对待，装上护栏（权限、分支、沙箱），这些企业级数据库几十年前就为人类做好了。被问到 agent 和坐在键盘前的人类是否有本质区别，他诚实地回答：除了 24/7 不休息，他还没看出来。全期最有力的一段是银行案例：某大型银行把 text-to-SQL 从 60% 准确率做到 99.5%——代价是数年时间和数百万美元搭建语义层。开箱即用，你拿到的是那个 60%。

两处细节值回整期时间。其一，他的 dbdb.io 项目追踪开源数据库的 co-signed 提交：超过 60% 的开源数据库现在有来自 Claude 或 Codex 的提交。其二，已经有两家数据库公司私下问他，怎么让自己的系统在 agent 那里被推荐到 Postgres 前面——"这基本上就是 SEO"，一门顾客是模型的新营销学科。他形容 AI 的速度："就像给猎豹喂大量可卡因，再把它塞进法拉利。"

其他留档观点：向量数据库只能以超专业化系统（Turbopuffer）或通用存储的形态存活——"Postgres 会死于向量数据库之手……不会发生的"；图数据库是"一个可怕的主意"（他和人打了赌：2030 年前图数据库市场若超过关系型，他就终身穿"我爱图数据库"的 T 恤）；传统 DBA 里调参数、选索引的活"该走了"，数据建模会留下来。
