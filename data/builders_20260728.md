**The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck**
*"OpenAI's Compute Chief: We Can't Build Fast Enough | Sachin Katti"*

Sachin Katti, head of industrial compute at OpenAI, paints a staggering picture of what it takes to power modern AI. OpenAI is planning roughly $50 billion in compute spend this year; the industry as a whole is approaching $700 billion. Katti describes AI data centers as "giant factories that are turning electrons into tokens"—football-field-sized facilities with liquid-cooled supercomputers where even the cables and transformers need cooling.

The conversation covers the full stack: why OpenAI is now designing its own chips (Jalapeno, optimized for tokens-per-watt), how the new MRC networking protocol masks failures across 100,000+ GPU clusters, and why the company is investing directly in power generation and grid infrastructure wherever it builds. Katti reveals that inference is already the majority of compute usage—and increasingly, "training is inference" because synthetic data generation and post-training all run inference workloads.

On the most debated question—are they overbuilding?—Katti's answer is blunt: "Demand far outstrips compute supply today. So anything we can bring online, we consume immediately." The real risk, he says, isn't having too much compute; it's that "the physical world does not move that fast" and supply chains can't keep up. He also confirms that AI is already assisting in chip design, and believes "the world of recursion is not that far where AI will design the systems it needs to train and run the next generation of AI."
