### AI & I by Every — Microsoft's Vision for an Internet Made for Agents

**Guest:** Kevin Scott, CTO of Microsoft

**The Takeaway:** The biggest story in AI right now isn't scaling laws or model benchmarks — it's building the "agentic web," an open ecosystem where agents can take actions on behalf of users across systems, just as HTTP enabled the web.

Microsoft CTO Kevin Scott, in a wide-ranging interview, laid out his vision for where AI is heading. He argues that scaling laws have become a settled question — "they are intact and working quite well" — and that a bigger challenge is the "capability overhang": models have gotten ahead of what products are actually delivering to users. The industry needs to close this gap.

The dominant theme of the conversation was the agentic web. Scott draws a direct parallel between today's AI infrastructure and the early internet: MCP is like HTTP (the protocol layer), and NL web (natural language web) is like HTML (the content layer). For agents to be truly useful, they need to be able to use tools, make changes in systems, and consult diverse information sources — which requires an open, permissionless ecosystem.

On security, Scott acknowledges MCP's current limitations but argues its simplicity makes it "relatively easy for the community to decide what the security model is." Key priorities: agent identities, entitlement systems, and user-controlled permission flows.

Perhaps the most personal section addressed the debate over AI-assisted coding and "craft." Scott, a programmer for 41 years and avid woodworker, compares today's agent-coding skeptics to historical debates about power tools vs. hand tools, and now CNC vs. traditional power tools. His advice: "Be curious. Try stuff. And if it works for you, use it. And if it doesn't, don't."

Scott predicts that within a year, agents will shift from synchronous "prompt and wait" interactions to asynchronous delegation — "Hey, go sort this out" — where agents make multiple calls, integrate responses, and return hours later with a complete result.

微软 CTO Kevin Scott 在这次深度访谈中勾勒了 AI 的下一步走向。他认为扩展定律已成为定论——「它们 intact 且运转良好」——更大的挑战是「能力悬空」：模型能力已经跑到了产品交付的前面，整个行业需要缩小这一差距。

访谈的核心主题是「代理网络」（agentic web）。Scott 将今天的 AI 基础设施与早期互联网做了直接类比：MCP 相当于 HTTP（协议层），NL web（自然语言网页）相当于 HTML（内容层）。要让代理真正有用，它们必须能够使用工具、在系统中做出更改、并查询多样化的信息源——这需要一个开放、无需许可的生态系统。

在安全方面，Scott 承认 MCP 目前存在不足，但认为其简洁性使得「社区相对容易地决定安全模型应该是什么」。优先事项包括：代理身份、权限体系、以及用户控制的授权流程。

访谈中最个人化的部分是关于 AI 辅助编程与「工匠精神」的争论。Scott 是一位拥有 41 年编程经验的热衷木工，他将今天对代理编程的质疑者与历史上关于电动工具 vs. 手工工具、以及现在 CNC vs. 传统电动工具的争论相提并论。他的建议：「保持好奇。尝试新事物。如果适合你，就用。不适合，就不用。」

Scott 预测，一年内代理将从同步的「提示-等待」交互模式转向异步委托——「嘿，去搞定这个」——代理会发起多次调用、整合响应，并在数小时后返回完整结果。
