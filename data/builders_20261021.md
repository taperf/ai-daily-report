### AI & I by Every — "Why Every Traded Personal Agents for One Company Agent"

The Takeaway: Every's eight-month journey from "everyone gets a personal agent" to "one company agent for everyone" is the most honest case study yet on how agent adoption actually plays out inside a real organization — and it ends with a split prediction: one company agent at work, one personalized agent at home.

Dan Shipper (Every CEO) and Willie Williams (Every's head of platform) walk through the full arc: the OpenClaw phase where every employee got a personal agent, the Slack channel full of bots ("absolute chaos"), the security and maintenance burden, the failed "Plus One" platform bet, and the collective realization that the company should pool all that energy into a single agent. They just launched that company agent — the Every Agent — to all subscribers.

The episode's sharpest insights are refreshingly specific. On agent attachment: personal agents inspire real emotional bonds, but Shipper's honest read is that personality is disposable — "as soon as something better comes along, you throw away the cute little character you develop." His own agent names (R2C2, and his OpenAI dot "Boo") all get RIP'd. On the org split: Williams argues "it's gonna be one agent per company or org structure" at work because a personal agent "needs to have been there a year to really get up to speed," while the personal side keeps getting more attractive as consumer agents mature. On engineering management in the AI age, Williams' counterintuitive observation: his token usage "fell off a cliff" when he moved back into management — the job is still fundamentally a people job, but his leverage increased "because their leverage has increased." Agents now sit on-call 24/7 as the first responder, watch dashboards every five minutes for anomalies, and feed him a consolidated view via Tend.

One external data point worth flagging: this is week two of OpenAI's Dots, and Shipper's verdict after daily use — filtering Slack noise is the killer feature, but "the whole permission structure of Dots is completely a mystery to me and is extremely annoying."

核心结论：Every 从"人人一个个人 agent"到"全公司一个 company agent"的八个月历程，是迄今关于 agent 在真实组织内落地方式最诚实的一份案例研究——而且它以一个分裂式预测收尾：上班用公司 agent，回家用个人 agent。

Dan Shipper（Every CEO）和 Willie Williams（Every 平台负责人）完整复盘了这段弧线：OpenClaw 阶段人人配一个个人 agent、Slack 里全是机器人（"绝对的混乱"）、安全与维护负担、失败的"Plus One"平台豪赌，以及最终的集体觉醒——应该把所有这些精力汇入一个全公司共享的 agent。这个 company agent（Every Agent）刚刚向全部订阅用户发布。

这期最锋利的洞见都具体得罕见。关于对 agent 的情感依恋：个人 agent 确实会激发真实的感情联结，但 Shipper 的诚实判断是——人设是可抛弃的，"一旦出现更好的东西，你会毫不犹豫地扔掉你精心养出来的那个可爱角色"。他自己的 agent 们（R2C2、OpenAI 的 dot"Boo"）全都 RIP 了。关于组织形态的分裂：Williams 认为职场侧"会是每个公司或组织一个 agent"，因为个人 agent"得在公司待满一年才能真正进入状态"；而消费级个人 agent 随着大厂入局会越来越有吸引力，家庭侧会持续升温。关于 AI 时代的工程管理，Williams 的反直觉观察：转回管理岗后他的 token 消耗"断崖式下跌"——这份工作本质上仍是管人的工作，但他的杠杆变大了，"因为他们（工程师）的杠杆变大了"。现在 agent 全天候值班当第一响应人、每五分钟盯一次仪表盘找异常，再通过 Tend 把整合后的信息流喂给他。

一个值得单独划出的外部数据点：这是 OpenAI Dots 发布的第二周，Shipper 每天使用后的评价——过滤 Slack 噪音是杀手级功能，但"Dots 的整个权限结构对我来说完全是个谜，而且极其烦人"。
