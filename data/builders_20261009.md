**Aditya Agarwal (General Partner at SPC, ex-Dropbox CTO)**

The best long-form essay in today's feed: what should an ambitious software engineer do today? Ten years ago, great engineering opened doors everywhere; now "I can build it" is a weak moat because AI makes producing working software widely available. He lays out three paths: (1) work on the thing that's eating software, meaning AI research and the infrastructure that makes new capabilities useful, where technical ability remains a real constraint; (2) team up with a world-class domain expert, since fifteen years of industry knowledge about where mistakes are expensive and why past fixes failed is hard to acquire from outside; (3) shepherd agents writing "vanilla code" inside a company, which he finds least compelling: "if your ambition extends only to completing the same backlog faster, you're underusing the moment." His closing advice: stay great at engineering, but be deliberate about what that ability sits next to, and use the fact that you can build more to take responsibility for something bigger.

今日信息量最大的一篇长文：一个有野心的软件工程师今天该做什么？十年前，出色的工程能力几乎在所有行业都是稀缺通行证；如今“我能做出来”不再是护城河，因为 AI 让做出能用的软件变得人人可得。他给出三条路：一，去做“正在吃掉软件的那个东西”，即 AI 研究和让新能力真正可用的基础设施，那里技术能力仍是硬约束；二，和世界级的领域专家组队，十五年行业积累知道哪些错误代价高昂、为什么以前的修复都失败了，这种知识从外部很难获得；三，在公司里带一群 agent 写“普通代码”，这条他觉得最没意思：“如果你的野心只是更快地清完同样的 backlog，你浪费了这个时代。”结尾建议：继续把工程能力练到极强，但要慎重选择让这种能力与什么为邻，并用“你能做更多”去承担更大的事。

**AI & I by Every: "Why Every Traded Personal Agents for One Company Agent"**

The Takeaway: personal agents create attachment and excitement, but companies get far more durable value from a single, shared, well-maintained company agent, because maintenance burden, security, and setup complexity kill individual agents over time.

Dan Shipper (CEO of Every) and Willie Williams (Every's head of platform) trace Every's full arc with agents: the OpenClaw wave that swept the company in January, the chaos of dozens of personal bots in Slack, the "Plus One" experiment to let anyone spin up their own agent, and the eventual collapse of nearly all personal agents. The most counterintuitive insight: people genuinely loved their personal agents (Shipper was attached to his, named R2C2), yet when a better option came along, they just let them die. "There's all these people in the org who don't really want to maintain their own claws, and the people who do want to do it, you kind of get tired of it if there's a better option." A second sharp observation: in AI products, the surface area is unbounded, so builder types want the knobs and complexity, while operators just want something solid that works, and over time Every chose solid. The single shared agent became the gravitational center: the growth agent Montane became so good that others stopped investing in their own and just contributed to it. Seeing colleagues actually use AI in Slack turned out to be the biggest unlock of all: "telling people how to use AI lands flat; showing them transforms." Every ultimately shipped its company agent, the Every Agent, to all subscribers.

核心结论：个人 agent 能带来情感依恋和新鲜感，但公司真正持久的价值来自一个统一维护、全员共享的公司级 agent，因为维护负担、安全问题和配置复杂度最终会杀死一个个独立的个人 agent。

Dan Shipper（Every CEO）和 Willie Williams（Every 平台负责人）复盘了 Every 的完整 agent 演进之路：一月份席卷全公司的 OpenClaw 浪潮、Slack 里几十个个人 bot 的混乱局面、让任何人一键创建 agent 的“Plus One”实验，以及几乎所有个人 agent 的最终消亡。最反直觉的发现是：大家真心喜欢自己的个人 agent（Shipper 对他那个叫 R2C2 的 agent 很有感情），但更好的选项一出现，他们就任由其死掉。“公司里很多人其实不想维护自己的 agent；而那些愿意折腾的人，一旦出现更好的选择也会疲惫放手。”第二个锐利观察：AI 产品的表面积是无边界的，builder 型的人喜欢暴露复杂度和可调的旋钮，运营型的人只想要一个稳定能用的东西，而 Every 最终选择了稳定。共享的单一 agent 会成为引力中心：增长团队的 agent Montane 做得太好，其他人干脆停止投入自己的 agent，转而共同维护它。而最大的解锁来自“看见”：“告诉别人怎么用 AI 是苍白的，让他们亲眼看到才真正有冲击力。”Every 最终把公司级 agent 做成了产品 Every Agent，开放给全部订阅用户。
