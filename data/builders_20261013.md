### No Priors：Frontier Chips for Frontier AI Labs —— 对话 Fractile 创始人兼 CEO Walter Goodwin

**The Takeaway:** The AI chip race won't be won by building a better GPU — it'll be won by whoever solves memory bandwidth for inference, because that's the one axis where the industry has barely moved in twenty years.

Walter Goodwin founded Fractile in mid-2022 to build full-stack inference chips for the world's largest models, and his read of the landscape is quietly brutal. Crack open an NVIDIA system and you find six to nine custom chips; look across the "extraordinary zoo" of accelerators — Google TPU, Meta MTIA, Microsoft Maya, OpenAI's in-house efforts — and you find near-identikit designs: the same HBM memory, the same tensor cores, the same TSMC advanced packaging, mostly delivered through a small number of ASIC houses like Broadcom (a $2 trillion company). In his telling, the first-party chips' primary purpose is often just price leverage against NVIDIA. Fractile's counter-bet is owning the entire stack in-house — architecture through physical design through packaging — with only about 150 people, which lets them run an agile closed loop that the handoff-based model can't.

Fractile's own journey is instructive: they spent two years on an SRAM-based design like Groq's, then abandoned it when context lengths started exploding, because SRAM's tiny capacity can't hold long-context state. Their new platform, ramping in the second half of next year, pairs GPU-class DRAM capacity with Groq-class bandwidth. Goodwin's sharpest framing: the "snappier chatbot" is the faster horses of fast inference — the real prize is long-running agents, where running multi-trillion-parameter models at thousands of tokens per second is a capability elevator. And the economics collapse to something mundane: cost per gigabyte of memory.

Two numbers worth memorizing: FLOPs have scaled roughly a million-fold in twenty years; memory bandwidth has scaled about 40x. That asymmetry is why he believes in "scaling laws for bandwidth" — sparser MoEs (1-in-128 or 1-in-256) and bandwidth-heavy attention schemes are all throttled on HBM hardware today, and a chip that fixes this unlocks capability, not just speed. On market structure, his most contrarian point: frontier labs structurally cannot go all-in on proprietary silicon, because if a rival lab finds a computational breakthrough that only runs on a different chip, you could die in the nine months before you can redeploy. Everyone at the frontier must run the same platforms as everyone else — which is exactly the gap an independent third-party chip vendor can occupy. His rule of thumb for winning deployments: structurally carve out a three-to-six-month advantage.

**核心要点：** AI 芯片竞赛的赢家不会是「做出更好 GPU」的人，而是解决推理侧内存带宽的人——因为这是过去二十年行业几乎没动过的那条轴。

Walter Goodwin 于 2022 年年中创立 Fractile，做面向超大规模模型的全栈推理芯片。他对行业格局的判断相当犀利：拆开一台 NVIDIA 的机器，里面有六到九颗自研芯片；再看整个「蔚为壮观的动物园」——Google TPU、Meta MTIA、微软 Maya、OpenAI 的自研项目——其实都是近似复制品：同样的 HBM、同样的 tensor core、同样的台积电先进封装，大多经由 Broadcom（市值两万亿美元）这类 ASIC 代交付厂商落地。按他的说法，云厂商自研芯片的首要功能往往只是对 NVIDIA 的议价筹码。Fractile 的对冲式下注是全栈自研——从架构到物理设计到封装——而且只有约 150 人，这让他们能跑一个「交接式」模式跑不了的敏捷闭环。

Fractile 自己的路线演化很有信息量：前两年他们做的是类似 Groq 的 SRAM 方案，后来因为上下文长度爆发而放弃——SRAM 容量太小，装不下长上下文状态。他们预计明年下半年量产的新平台，把 GPU 级的 DRAM 容量和 Groq 级的带宽结合在了一起。他最锋利的一个比喻：更快的聊天回复是快速推理领域的「更快的马」——真正的奖品是长时间运行的 agent，让数万亿参数模型以每秒数千 token 的速度奔跑，这是能力层面的电梯。而推理的经济学最后会塌缩到一件很朴素的事上：每 GB 内存的成本。

两个值得记住的数字：过去二十年，FLOPs 大约提升了一百万倍；内存带宽只提升了约 40 倍。正是这个不对称让他相信存在「带宽的 scaling law」——更稀疏的 MoE（1/128、1/256）和各种带宽饥渴的 attention 方案，今天都被 HBM 硬件卡着脖子；能解决这个问题的芯片解锁的不只是速度，而是能力本身。关于市场格局，他最反共识的一点是：前沿实验室在结构上不可能全押自研芯片——如果对手在另一种芯片上找到了计算效率的突破口，你可能在完成重新部署之前的九个月里就死了。所有前沿玩家都必须和彼此跑同样的平台，而这正是一个独立第三方芯片厂商可以占据的缝隙。他给出的取胜法则：只要能在结构上 carved out 三到六个月的领先，你就能赢下所有部署。
