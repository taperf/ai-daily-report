### Garry Tan — Y Combinator President & CEO

Tan is campaigning against California's Prop 40, which he calls a dishonest "asset seizure tax." His math: a founder who owns 50% of the company with 20x voting rights owes 5% on $10B, which means owing $500M out of a paper $1B net worth. Try getting a loan for half your illiquid net worth — you can't. So while proponents pitch it as a 5% tax on startups, in this simple case it's effectively a 50% tax. He also blasts the academics behind the policy for not understanding what happens to startups or "how new innovations occur and how much of a miracle that is for CA," arguing their ignorance produces misguided policy that will kill the golden goose.

Tan 正在公开反对加州 40 号提案，他称之为不诚实的“资产没收税”。他算了一笔账：一个持有公司 50% 股份、拥有 20 倍投票权的创始人，按 100 亿美元资产征 5%，就要从账面 10 亿美元净资产中拿出 5 亿现金。而你想用一半的非流动性净资产去借一笔贷款？借不到。所以支持者嘴上说这是对创业公司征 5% 的税，在这个简单例子里实际等效于 50%。他还炮轰提案背后的学界人士根本不懂创业公司的处境，也“不懂新创新如何诞生、这对加州是多么奇迹般的存在”，认为这种无知会产生杀死下金蛋鹅的错误政策。

### Nikunj Kothari — FPV Ventures Partner（早期 Meter、Opendoor、Atlassian 员工）

Kothari argues venture investing is overcomplicated yet deceptively simple. The formula: 1) win the right to, 2) find and invest in exceptional founders, 3) just before it becomes "consensus," 4) consistently every year. The vast majority of VCs do maybe one of these right; the truly great do ALL of them right. The least-discussed part is consistency — staying hungry regardless of track record, wealth, misses or TVPI. His metaphor is Djokovic: enjoying hitting the ball every day, always focused on the next point, because the next opportunity might go to someone who wanted it more.

Kothari 认为风险投资被过度复杂化了，本质上简单得惊人。公式是：一、赢得资格；二、找到杰出的创始人并投进去；三、时机刚好在形成“共识”之前；四、每年都稳定做到。绝大多数 VC 顶多做对其中一条，真正做到伟大的必须全部做对。最少被谈论的是“持续性”：无论过往战绩、财富积累、错过多少机会、当前基金的 TVPI 多少，都要保持饥饿感。他的比喻是 Djokovic：享受每天击球，永远专注下一个得分点，因为下一个机会可能会属于那个比你更想要它的人。

### How Hugging Face's Investor Spots Winners — AI & I by Every

**The Takeaway:** The edge in early-stage investing isn't predicting the exact future — it's finding "the newly technically possible," then backing technical founders whose pivot options aren't constrained by a vertical market.

Matt Hartman, founder and managing partner of Factorial Capital and the first investor in Hugging Face (he wrote the check while a partner at Betaworks, before the company became the "GitHub for AI" and was eventually bought by NVIDIA for $13B), opens with a story most people get wrong. Hugging Face started as a consumer chatbot for teenagers — with honest-to-bad retention. Instead of raising on vanity metrics, the team was brutally honest with itself, open-sourced its PyTorch port of the Transformers paper, watched it go viral on GitHub, and followed the demand to become the largest home for open source AI. "A thousand great decisions," Hartman says, but the constants were open source and staying flexible about what kinds of models they hosted.

His investing method is refreshingly concrete: find what's newly programmable (word2vec in 2015, Transformers after that, robotics and science models now), then ask as many smart people as possible. He filters founders through the basketball "pivot foot" rule — assume every early company will pivot, and ask whether the pivot can go anywhere the technology leads (technical founders) or is trapped inside one market (vertical executives). He also hunts for "earned insights": how many people in the world were even positioned to learn what you learned? If the answer is ten, you have time and head start.

Factorial itself is a structural experiment: a solo GP fund with venture partners including Hugging Face CEO Clem Delangue, a Venmo co-founder, and Hilary Mason. The flywheel is real — HF led him to Nomic, Nomic to Patronus, Patronus to Causal Labs, a family tree of technical founders referring each other. And he practices what he preaches: he vibe-coded a local voice-to-text tool called Ghost Pepper using Claude and Jesse Vincent's "superpowers" skill, hit #1 on Hacker News, and open-sourced it — while learning the humbling lesson that vibe-coded tools still need maintenance, which is precisely why SaaS products that keep iterating deserve their price.

His most memorable line: "The best people I've seen building things are playing. We are in an era that reminds me of early Internet — everyone is playing." Today's themes he's pulling on: maintenance tools for the new developer population, frontier non-language models (robotics, weather, drug repurposing), and full-stack application companies that can shave milliseconds off server time with a design decision, like Instagram's square photos did.

**核心观点：** 早期投资的护城河不在于精确预测未来，而在于找到“新近变得技术上可行”的东西，然后押注那些转型方向不被垂直市场锁死的技术型创始人。

Matt Hartman 是 Factorial Capital 创始人兼管理合伙人，也是 Hugging Face 的第一个投资人（当时他还是 Betaworks 合伙人，远在 HF 成为“AI 界 GitHub”、最终被 NVIDIA 以 130 亿美元收购之前）。他纠正了一个大众误解：Hugging Face 起初是给青少年用的聊天机器人，留存率很差。但团队没有靠虚荣指标去融资，而是对自己极度诚实，把 Transformers 论文转写成 PyTorch 版本并开源，在 GitHub 上爆火，随后顺着需求成为全球最大的开源 AI 社区。Hartman 说他们做了“一千个正确决定”，但不变量是坚持开源、对托管什么类型的模型保持灵活。

他的投资方法非常具体：找到“新近可编程”的东西（2015 年是 word2vec，之后是 Transformer，现在是机器人和科学模型），然后尽可能多地向聪明人提问。他筛创始人用篮球“轴心脚”规则——默认每家早期公司都会转型，问题在于转型是被技术引向任何方向（技术型创始人），还是被困在一个垂直市场里（行业高管型创始人）。他还寻找“挣来的洞察”（earned insight）：全世界有多少人有机会学到你学到的东西？如果答案是十个人，你就有时间和先发优势。

Factorial 本身就是一场结构性实验：solo GP 基金加上一众 venture partner，包括 Hugging Face CEO Clem Delangue、Venmo 联合创始人、Hilary Mason。飞轮效应真实存在——HF 引荐了 Nomic，Nomic 引荐 Patronus，Patronus 引荐 Causal Labs，一张技术创始人的家族树互相引荐。他自己也在身体力行：用 Claude 加上 Jesse Vincent 的“superpowers”技能，vibe code 了一个本地语音转文字工具 Ghost Pepper，冲上 Hacker News 第一名并开源——同时收获一个谦卑的教训：vibe code 出来的工具仍需维护，这正是那些持续迭代的 SaaS 产品配得上其定价的原因。

最值得记住的一句话：“我见过的最出色的建造者都在玩。我们正处在一个让我想起早期互联网的时代，所有人都在玩。”他当下在拉动的主题包括：面向新开发者人群的维护工具、前沿非语言模型（机器人、气象、老药新用），以及能像 Instagram 方形照片那样用设计决策省下服务器毫秒的全栈应用公司。
