**AI & I by Every：每家公司都需要一个“前沿团队”（Frontier Team）**

*The Takeaway:* Keeping up with AI is now a full-time job — so Every made it one. Their "frontier team" exists to chase new models full-time so that nobody else in the company has to, and their biggest lesson is organizational, not technical: separate the people who explore from the people who execute.

Dan Shipper (CEO of Every) dedicated a full episode to reverse-engineering his own company's frontier team — the unit he spun up a few months ago that has, in his words, been "kind of transformative." The core dilemma: every person in a company is caught between "do my actual work" and "stay current with AI," and trying to do both produces frazzled, distracted employees. The frontier team resolves this by separation of concerns: its members run experiments at the edge, the best results get pushed into products and services, and the rest of the company only pays attention when something is actually ready for them. The most useful framework from the episode: frontier teams need both "pirates" — people who move fast, break things, and try a billion sloppy experiments in days — and "architects," who compress that mess into maintainable systems. Most experiments get thrown away; the architect decides what survives.

The most counterintuitive insights came from the team's evals lead Mike Taylor, who argued there's still massive "capability overhang" — models can do far more than anyone has had time to try. His demo: he had Opus (in its highest-effort mode, orchestrating hundreds of agents, one per meeting) read all his Slack messages and meeting notes, distill the narrative of a product explainer into 12 auditable sentences with receipts, then cut the entire video — annotated screenshots, voiceover and all. His advice cuts both ways: "just make that leap" and give AI access to all your context, because it's remarkably good at picking what matters; but once you start producing more, protecting other people's context becomes your job too. Operator Arielle (the "Frontier Wrangler") built a token-spend dashboard across every AI harness in the company with no engineers, noting that her capacity to build systems now grows as models get smarter. And Kieran Klaassen's advice to anyone running such a team: a hundred experiments dumped into a channel will just overwhelm people — "make sure some stick... it is about integration."

*最值得记住的一句话：* “There's still a lot of capability overhang — the models are able to do much more than we have had time to try.”（能力过剩仍然严重——模型能做的事，远多于我们有时间去尝试的。）

**中文摘要：**

Dan Shipper（Every CEO）用一整期节目拆解了自己公司几个月前成立的“前沿团队”——用他的话说，这个团队“带来了变革性的改变”。核心困境是：公司里每个人都被夹在“干好本职工作”和“跟上 AI 前沿”之间，两头兼顾只会让人焦头烂额。前沿团队的解法是关注点分离：团队成员在边缘疯狂做实验，最好的成果被推入产品和服务，而公司其他人只在东西真正成熟时才需要关注。节目里最有用的框架：前沿团队需要两种人——“海盗”（跑得飞快、几天做一万个粗糙实验的人）和“建筑师”（把混乱压缩成可维护系统的人），大多数实验会被扔掉，建筑师决定什么能活下来。

最反直觉的洞察来自评测负责人 Mike Taylor：他认为“能力过剩”（capability overhang）仍然严重——模型能做的事远超我们有时间去尝试的范围。他的演示堪称本期高光：让 Opus（最高投入模式，调度数百个 agent、每个会议一个）读完他所有的 Slack 消息和会议记录，把产品讲解视频的叙事提炼成 12 句可溯源、可审计的句子，然后完整剪出视频——连截图标注和配音都由 AI 完成。他的建议是双向的：大胆跨出那一步，把全部上下文交给 AI，它挑选重点的能力好得惊人；但当你产出变多之后，“保护别人的注意力”也变成了你工作的一部分。“前沿牧人” Arielle 不写代码却为公司搭起了横跨所有 AI 工具的 token 开销仪表盘，她的感受是：模型越强，她构建系统的能力就越强。Kieran Klaassen 给同行的忠告则很务实：往频道里倒一百个实验只会把所有人淹没——要让一些成果真正落地，落地方向的整合才是前沿团队的本职。
